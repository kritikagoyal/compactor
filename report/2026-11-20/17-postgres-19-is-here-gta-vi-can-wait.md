## Summary
#​674 — November 19, 2026 Web Version Postgres Weekly Postgres 19 Released It might be GTA VI's release day, but we all know the day's true big release… Postgres 19 is here ! It's headlined by online table repacking and new ways to keep query plans stable. Some highlights: REPACK reclaims bloat and can reorder a table to match an index, and REPACK (CONCURRENTLY) does it without blocking reads or writes until the final swap. It supersedes VACUUM FULL and CLUSTER (which remain for compatibility). For developers, INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO SELECT handles 'get or create' in one round trip, COPY can output JSON, and window functions gain IGNORE NULLS . The pg_plan_advice extension lets you experiment with and guide plan choices, and pg_stash_advice applies advice automatically by query ID, so you can fix a slow plan without changing your app. Logical replication can be turned on without a restart, sequences can now be replicated , and WAIT FOR LSN gives read-your-writes consistency on standbys. And, of course, much, much more , all covered in the release notes. Tiger Data (creators of TimescaleDB) sponsor BEFORE YOU UPGRADE TO POSTGRES 19: Indexes using full text search, pg_trgm or fuzzystrmatch may need a reindex after pg_upgrade . Jeff Davis explains why there's no upgrade check for it. pg_upgrade --check can pass and the upgrade still fail if a role or database stores standard_conforming_strings = off , or you've added your own uuid to bytea cast (now built in). After a pg_restore , run VACUUM ANALYZE . Nikolay saw a query take 32.6s instead of 23ms without it, and the first read after a restore can be costly (on 19, particularly). JIT is now off by default , as its cost estimates proved unreliable. If you run a lot of large analytical queries, you may want to turn it back on.
